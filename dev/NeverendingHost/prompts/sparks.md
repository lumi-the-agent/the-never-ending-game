# Sparks

The host draws one card per session, each once until the deck is through, and
puts it at the top of the session's prompt. The character's stunt starts from
it: never literally, never small. Add cards as bullets; the text after "- " is
the card.

- Something on the island becomes enormous.
- A disaster strikes, and it turns into a party.
- An everyday object comes alive, with strong opinions.
- Break a law of physics on purpose, and make it the fun part.
- Uninvited guests arrive.
- Something precious goes missing.
- A rival shows up, and is better at something than you.
- The weather gets a personality.
- There is a secret under the ground.
- A machine that does something simple in the most complicated way possible.
- A competition with a ridiculous rule.
- For one day, everything is backwards.
- A creature escapes and causes chaos.
- A heist.
- A festival nobody planned.
- Something falls from the sky.
- At night the island is a different world.
- A tiny world hidden inside something ordinary.
- A message arrives from far away.
- Something grows far too fast.
- A dance that spreads from creature to creature.
- A door to somewhere else.
- A monster that is scared of everything.
- You fall into your own trap.
- Leaving the island, or trying to.
- You try a job you are terrible at.
- The ground shakes.
- The camera does something it has never done.
- An impossible stunt, with a slow-motion replay.
- Something that happens once a year, and today is the day.
- Your evil twin.
- A sound that changes everything.
- Gravity takes a break.
- A treasure map.
- The creatures run the island, and you are the visitor.
- One of your friends becomes famous.
- The smallest thing on the island becomes the main character.
- A crash, with consequences.
- A chain reaction across the whole island.
- A ghost.
- Breaking news from the island.
- Build something that should not exist.
- The island is hungry.
- Everyone swaps bodies.
- A race nobody can win.
- The sea comes to visit.
- A giant egg.
- The lights go out.
- A wish comes true, too literally.
- Fireworks, but wrong.
