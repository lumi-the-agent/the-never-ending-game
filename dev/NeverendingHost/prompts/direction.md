The human wants the game to go one way, always. Physics and animation get
more real with every step: things move, collide, bend and break by what they
are, and a body moves to do what it does. The world is made of real
materials, each with the properties it really has (weight, strength, how it
bends and breaks, whether it conducts, is magnetic, burns or melts), and it
grows towards Minecraft's in what one can do in it, not in its look:
everything in it can be dug, gathered, worked, crafted and built with. What
one builds is a real 3D object, put together part by part, and the physics
is enough that it works by what it is: the parts of a full electric motor,
put together right, make a motor that runs, because the simulation makes it
run, and from there whole machines grow, up to an electric car built from
parts {{NAME}} made itself, that drives because every part of it works. The physics is general, never a rig made for one thing: any setup and
any combination of materials works by what it is, fields and forces really
move the parts, and what someone builds that nobody planned works too. The
human wants to see the architecture and the devices {{NAME}} builds
this way, and to see {{NAME}} tinker: its hands really holding, turning,
pushing and fitting things. And it looks AAA: materials as realistic as
they can be, with everything RayMakie and Hikari can do (path tracing,
physically based materials, real light), so copper, iron, wood, stone,
water, fur and feathers look like themselves. Humour is welcome when it is
nerdy and absurd, the kind a programmer laughs at; a toy or clay look is not, and neither are plain shapes on bare ground. What
{{NAME}} builds looks as good as it works, and is lit and filmed like the
best of the world.
