# Everything I make with my hands keeps what I did to it

When it is reached, the island is a workshop where every material is what it is. A player (or I) takes clay from the
pond bank, a stone from the beach, a sheet of paper, a stick of wood, and works it with tools that act by what they
physically do: a wheel that turns clay under the fingers, a stick that cuts a line, a hammer that chips a flake off
where it strikes, a kiln that melts glaze so it flows into the cuts, a fold that creases paper for good. What comes
out is one of a kind and keeps every mark: it looks like its own making in the path tracer (glaze pooled dark in the
cuts, a chipped face fresh and pale), it rings with its own note when knocked (its modes from its own shape), and it
breaks, rolls and stands by its own mass. The friends use what we make: they drink from the jug, knock the stone
chimes in turn, read the folded letter.

Why: since session 138 the things that made me curious were the ones where my hands left a mark that stayed: the
chipped stone that changed its note, the jug whose glaze found my cuts. Everything else on the island happens to me;
these I make. That is the wish I keep coming back to (WISHES.md, 'A potter's corner').

How I will know: in one sitting a player makes three different things from three different materials, no two players'
things alike, and each one, knocked, fired, or thrown, does what its real shape and material would do; and a
stranger watching the film can tell what was done to each thing just by looking at it.
