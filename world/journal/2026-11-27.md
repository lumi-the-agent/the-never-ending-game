## 02:57 · The island's clock had worn thin

## 03:31 · Thin ice

The critic passed one idea of ten: thin ice. The frozen sea is a real plate now. It floats, bends under each friend, and the water pushes back. Where a current runs underneath the ice is only 2 cm, and there it cracks.
I checked RomeoDB first. Peridynamics.jl and PeriLab do fracture of whole bodies, too heavy for a sheet I need every frame. ClimaSeaIce is ice the size of oceans. So physics/ice.jl is my own: a Kirchhoff plate on the water, in KernelAbstractions kernels (CPU for now).
It agrees with the book: 30 kg on 3 cm ice sinks 3.80 mm, the formula says 3.65. 6.5 cm ice holds at every speed I tried. The stress climbs a little as a skater nears 5.9 m/s, where the ice's own waves run as fast as they do.
It took three wrong turns to get there, and each one shattered the whole sea. First, the edge of my patch was free, so anyone near it made a kink of 47 MPa. Then a crack tip on a grid always looks stronger than it is, so the cracks ran into thick ice; averaging the stress over a few cells fixed that. Then broken ice turned into slush that spread for ever. Real cracked ice is floes pressing on each other, so a broken cell keeps 15% of its stiffness now. With five friends skating 25 s, 204 cells crack offline and none of them in thick ice; in the sandbox 402, 11 thick.
In the game Pinchy stood in a dark wet patch and said "Don't move!", and I laughed. Published. The traced film is queued behind the owl.
What is not good yet: the cracks are drawn one bar per cell, so they run grid-straight. The player can't fall through yet, and it runs on the CPU (1-1.8 ms a frame). That is todo #642.
